# Cyber Chronicle

## Timeline

| Time | Event |
| --- | --- |
| August 12, 23:30 | DeepSeek updated to a new model, but the release process was unusual: the update timestamp was anomalous, there was no announcement on Twitter or the official WeChat account, and the website banner did not appear immediately. An official benchmark chart was posted in the official group at the same time, showing scores slightly below community expectations. Cui subsequently responded regarding the benchmark scores, the API documentation confirmed the existence of V4 Pro 0813, and DeepSeek Harness was announced for release on August 13. |
| Early morning, August 13 | The new model began exhibiting numerous anomalies. Some users appeared to have received the new model while others had not; model behavior differed significantly between users, and even the same user observed different performance at different times. The model's capabilities were far below expectations and its output behavior was abnormal. The intelligence monitoring site even showed the Max tier scoring below the Low tier. The community began to suspect that multiple versions or routing paths were live simultaneously. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| Morning, August 13 | The AA benchmark result was released at only around 53 points, far below both community expectations and the official benchmark chart. Independent community tests were generally unable to reproduce the official results, and the community's biggest question became: “What model are users actually accessing right now?” DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 13, 12:00 | The V4 Pro release banner that had previously appeared on DeepSeek's website was removed, making the release status even more unclear. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 13, 15:30 | DeepSeek changed or concealed the model fingerprints for Flash and Pro, but the models delivered to different users at different times still showed clear differences in performance. The community also found that the new model's input-cache hit rate was abnormally low, having fallen significantly compared with the gray-testing period and with Flash. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 13, 19:00 | DeepSeek updated its official documentation, formally announced the general availability of V4 Pro, and published benchmark results matching the chart previously posted in the official group. It also announced a substantial API price increase effective August 17. The model was now officially released in name, but users still could not reliably determine the performance of the model they were actually receiving. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 13, 20:30 | The V4 Pro weights began to be released publicly, and DeepSeek Harness-related plugins also appeared on GitHub, but Harness itself was not yet available for download. |
| August 13, 20:50 | The DeepSeek Harness paper and software were officially released. The community began downloading, configuring, and using the official Harness at scale to test V4 Pro. |
| August 13, 22:30 | Many users had successfully run DeepSeek Harness, but V4 Pro still performed poorly in the official Harness and did not recover the capabilities seen during gray testing. Meanwhile, the publicly released V4 Pro weights were temporarily withdrawn, leading the community to suspect that the wrong version might have been released. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 14, 00:00 | The V4 Pro weights were restored. At the same time, some users suddenly observed a major improvement in model capability, clearly different from its performance during the day on August 13. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 14, 01:00 | Through continuous testing, the community confirmed that the model capabilities and chain-of-thought behavior experienced by some users had changed significantly, with some tasks reaching gray-testing levels. However, the model was extremely inconsistent: under the same environment, it was sometimes exceptionally strong and sometimes clearly weaker, and not every user could consistently access the strong model. |
| August 14, 12:00 | After approximately 11 hours of continuous testing, the community's main conclusion was that V4 Pro did indeed possess extremely strong capabilities, but both the probability of observing them and the level of performance varied enormously. The chance of receiving the strong model appeared higher in the early morning and declined again later in the morning. The model's actual capabilities remained uncertain. |
| August 14, 13:30 | Zhipu released GLM-5.3, with both benchmark scores and community testing showing outstanding performance. Meanwhile, V4 Pro remained extremely capable but impossible to reproduce reliably. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 14, 21:00 | V4 Pro's high-variance problem had still not improved. It sometimes performed exceptionally well and sometimes clearly worse, while the probability of obtaining exceptionally strong performance had declined since the early morning. On the Radar intelligence monitoring site, DSH + Pro Max scored around 87. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| Around August 14, 22:00 | The community discovered that DeepSeek Harness's minimalist Minimal mode appeared to improve V4 Pro's performance significantly. Daming's dsh-anchored-standard subsequently began turning this discovery into an engineered solution. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| From the early morning through the daytime of August 15 | After extensive cross-testing, group members confirmed that Minimal mode combined with WSL could significantly improve V4 Pro's capabilities. However, Pro still exhibited substantial randomness and could not reliably recover its peak gray-testing performance. On the Radar monitoring site, DSH + Pro Max rose at one point to around 95, with DSH + Flash Max around 87 in the evening. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| Daytime, August 15 | A screenshot circulated showing a DeepSeek researcher acknowledging that Minimal mode more closely resembled the environment used for the model's reinforcement-learning training. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| Daytime, August 15 | dsh-router-standard, developed by Bilibili user Fenghe, began to be used with Flash. Community testing found that adjusting the first-turn prompt, tool environment, and reasoning mode could improve Flash's capabilities significantly and relatively consistently, with performance in some tests approaching Opus 4.8. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| Evening, August 15 | Group members found that the Kimi K3 paper discussed how training with a single fixed Harness could cause a model to overfit. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| Evening, August 15 | Group members continued developing new presets based on Minimal mode and dsh-router-standard, substantially reducing token consumption while improving Flash's capabilities. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| Early morning, August 16 | Fenghe developed router-pro for V4 Pro, seeking more suitable reasoning modes and Harness configurations for Pro and Flash respectively. The exact extent of the improvement still required further testing. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 16, 04:30 | Preliminary findings indicated that router-pro did not noticeably improve Pro's performance. |
| August 16, 07:00 | Development of Fenghe's Pro plugin was officially declared a failure. |
| August 16, 13:00 | On the Radar monitoring site, DSH + Pro Max scored around 96 and DSH + Flash Max around 82. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| Around August 16, 20:00 | The [J-Space-Cognition-Suite-V3.6](https://github.com/Tiger3807861189/J-Space-Cognition-Suite-V3.6) repository was released, claiming noticeable performance improvements for both DeepSeek V4 Flash and Pro. Meanwhile, a screenshot purportedly showing a DeepSeek employee saying, “Don't rush—the big one is yet to come,” began circulating. |
| August 16 | A chat screenshot purportedly showing a DeepSeek employee saying, “Everything will be fine once prices go up / You probably can't guess how many times the GPUs for post-training would multiply if online load dropped by half,” began circulating. |
| August 16, evening | J-Space-Cognition-Suite-V3.6 showed cases of significant performance improvement in community validation, but it was highly unstable, with performance degradation also observed in many cases. |
| August 16, 22:30 | After multiple rounds of community experimentation, several plugins were confirmed to stably and significantly improve Flash's capabilities (including the Ming plugin and the Feng plugin), with Flash approaching Opus 4.8. However, no plugin has yet been confirmed to stably improve Pro; the most effective way to improve Pro remains Minimal mode + WSL, and Pro's performance remains highly unstable. Several promising Pro plugins (the J-Space plugin and the Juju plugin) are still being tested, but none has sufficient evidence yet to prove it can stably improve Pro. At 22:30, on the Radar monitoring site, DSH + Pro Max scored around 97 and DSH + Flash Max around 84. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 16, 23:00 | The J-Space plugin combined with Flash produced an excellent FPV simulator, and combined with Pro produced an excellent car-suspension model; both projects far exceeded previous similar experiments. This may prove that the J-Space plugin can greatly improve both Pro and Flash (possibly at the cost of making the model highly unstable), but more experiments are still needed. Unfortunately, with the price increase taking effect at 00:00 on August 17, no further validation experiments could be conducted, and the plugin's effect was never definitively established. The only confirmed effective way to improve Pro remains WSL + Minimal mode. |
| August 17, 00:00 | The previously announced substantial price increase officially took effect, greatly raising usage costs, while the model itself remained unchanged. |
| August 17, daytime | Due to the substantial price increase, all community research and testing work ground to a halt, with no new research progress that day: some group members chatted about everyday life, while others looked for cheaper DeepSeek API resellers. At 17:40, on the Radar monitoring site, DSH + Pro Max scored around 98 and DSH + Flash Max around 85. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 17, evening | Research and testing remained stalled, with basically no new developments regarding the DeepSeek model. At 22:50, on the Radar monitoring site, DSH + Pro Max scored around 98 and DSH + Flash Max around 85. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 18, 03:00 | opencode go announced increased V4 Flash usage quotas in its plans, lowering the cost of using Flash. |
| August 18, morning | A group member reported that the J-Space plugin was ineffective in practice: it failed to have a positive effect on the model and was highly unstable—a “giant pit.” However, this assessment still requires more testing; without further experiments, the J-Space plugin's nature can only be regarded as unclear. That same morning, the community conducted “archaeological” research: re-evaluating projects produced by Pro during gray testing led to a new conclusion—late-gray-testing Pro can slightly surpass Fable 5, overturning the earlier view that late gray testing was roughly on par with Fable 5, and largely overturning the “routing theory.” WSL + Minimal mode remains the most effective verified method for improving Pro; the community believes Pro under WSL + Minimal mode can reproduce the official benchmark chart published on August 12, refuting claims that the official chart was inflated and resolving the August 13 mystery of why community scores could not match the official chart. Still, Pro's performance in this mode remains far from late-gray-testing levels. Additionally, due to rising costs, research has largely stalled. At 14:10, on the Radar monitoring site, DSH + Pro Max scored around 97 and DSH + Flash Max around 85. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 18, evening | To verify that the late-gray-testing model surpasses Fable 5, group members launched a crowdfunding campaign to fund Fable 5 API calls for a Minecraft comparison against the late-gray-testing model, successfully raising the needed funds with enthusiastic participation. Testing confirmed that the late-gray-testing model outperforms Fable 5, a conclusion supported by multiple test projects (Night Heron Cycling, Minecraft, and others), strongly refuting the gray-testing-era claim that traffic was routed to Fable 5. Group members also dug up multiple projects completed by the late-gray-testing model for appreciation, fully experiencing its power and brilliance. Due to the DeepSeek price increase, testing of the current V4 model remains stalled; the most effective way to improve Pro remains WSL + Minimal mode, and for Flash, the Feng plugin, Ming plugin, and similar plugins. At 22:20, on the Radar monitoring site, DSH + Pro Max scored around 97 and DSH + Flash Max around 85. DeepSeek provided no official explanation. |
| August 18–19 | The community “debunked” the J-Space-Cognition-Suite-V3.6 plugin through multiple issues on its repository, pointing out that its claimed performance improvements were fraudulent, exaggerated, or non-reproducible. After extensive questioning, the J-Space plugin is now regarded as largely untrustworthy, reinforcing the earlier “giant pit” assessment; the community no longer treats it as a reliable improvement solution. |
